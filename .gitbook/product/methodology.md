# Interview preparation methodology

This page captures the **domain** Headway digitizes: how Innowise prepares candidates for technical interviews. It is product context, not a UI spec — individual screens reference it from their PRDs.

## Goal

The point of the preparation table is **not** to accumulate random knowledge. The goal is to be ready for an interview **with minimal effort**. Material is prioritized by expected payoff: a small number of topics show up on interviews very often and weigh heavily on the final decision, while a long tail of topics appears rarely but still matters.

## Tracks: Fast → Growth → Deep

Topics are split into three priority iterations:

* **Fast-Track** — "What must I know first to even go to an interview?" The highest-frequency, highest-impact topics.
* **Growth-Track** — depth where interviews most often drill in; details and nuance.
* **Deep-Track** — coverage of rarer but potentially important topics, and polishing depth.

## Approaches: Check → Slice → Mock

Instead of grinding top-to-bottom through the table, preparation **interleaves** topics and alternates three session types:

| Format | Language | Content | CV + self-intro | Soft skills |
|--------|----------|---------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Check** | 🇷🇺 Russian | Pre-announced topics; quick recall-style Q&A from the table; corrections of inaccuracies | ❌ no | ❌ no |
| **Slice** | 🇬🇧 English | Re-answer previously covered questions, shuffled across already-studied sections (A+B → A+B+C+D …); simulate interview unpredictability; check depth and durability. May drift sideways into adjacent questions | ❌ no | ✅ at most one from the Soft Skills page |
| **Mock interview** | 🇬🇧 English | Full interview replica: self-intro, CV/experience questions, Soft Skills block, technical questions on covered material, optional live-coding | ✅ required | ✅ required |

* **Check** — a recurring meeting on known topics. The mentor announces topics a few days ahead and schedules it. Questions answered fully and correctly get a checkmark in the employee's column. The aim is to assemble the puzzle and learn to answer confidently and completely. Run in Russian to stay within time and keep focus on the answer, not translation. Recall **without** the cheat sheet — effortful retrieval makes knowledge stick longer. Version 3.0 of the table also introduces **interleaving**: instead of `AAA → BBB → CCC`, study `ABC → ABC → ABC`. Block study feels mastered but doesn't build the flexibility needed when questions drift.
* **Slice** — rarer than a Check. After finishing blocks A and B, stop and shuffle questions from A and B, answering in English. Then study C and D; the next Slice samples A, B, C, D. This **retrieval practice** in English forces the brain to ask "what kind of question is this?" and pull from long-term memory, which research shows improves long-term retention across the board.
* **Mock interview** — the stage where much of the table is already covered. Drills the real, stressful conditions: self-intro, random CV-experience questions, Soft Skills (from the Soft Skills page), a technical pass on covered sections, optional live-coding — all in English. The share of mocks grows toward the finish.

Interview outcomes depend heavily on the **behavioral** part, so **Soft Skills** is a critical part of preparation, not decoration. After mocks, give strict, substantive feedback — catching issues early is far cheaper than fixing them at the last minute.

## Recommended cadence

Frequency is set per-employee by the manager based on availability: ~3 sessions/week for benched employees, ~2 for project-busy ones. Example reading of the schedule: for a benched employee who finished Fast-Track, Growth-Track runs as 2 Checks → 1 Slice → 2 Checks → 1 Mock.

A **basic CV and self-intro**, approved by the manager, are expected within the first two weeks — so they can be refined calmly before mocks, and so the employee is ready if an interview request arrives on short notice.

## AI Answer

Each question carries a human-reviewed **AI Answer** (Russian and English) — a concise, pre-checked summary to start from and to use as a pre-prompt for an LLM. It is a cheat sheet, **not** a source of truth: models can be wrong or over-generalize, Android moves fast, and answers may miss corner cases. Verify disputed points against primary sources.
